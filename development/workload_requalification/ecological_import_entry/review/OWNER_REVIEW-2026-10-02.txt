**Partly. E20 is incorporating several earlier mechanical lessons correctly. The incomplete transfer is mainly about what happens after source exposure: selecting evidence for the actual repair, maintaining current understanding, and preventing administrative state from dominating the prompt.**

## What E20 is already applying correctly

The coverage implementation is not simply repeating earlier work.

E19 checked a minimum exposure requirement. E20 requires complete, continuous coverage of eleven files. The new code accumulates exact delivered ranges, detects gaps, binds them to file versions, preserves coverage through release, and keeps current editing authority separate. Its qualification actually replaces source groups, then reacquires the editing targets. That is a legitimate extension of the mechanism to a different task contract.  

It also avoids several errors already encountered elsewhere:

The 3060 document lab previously rejected valid ranges spanning consecutive indexed passages because they did not match a single stored address. E20 explicitly supports a displayed range whose exact contents come from several acquisition records; it does not demand one nonexistent receipt covering the whole merged range. These are not identical operations, but E20 respects the underlying lesson: **a valid logical extent need not have one storage carrier**.  

It measures actual native inputs and checks feedback delivery, rather than qualifying only in-memory structures. And it preserves the original graders while separately checking the known zero-file-count gap, rather than allowing passing historical tests to redefine the task. 

These are meaningful incorporations of prior lessons. **I do not see evidence that the coverage function needs another redesign before use.**

## 1. The largest incomplete transfer: inspection must lead to a decision-specific evidence group

The strongest relevant result from the 3060 document lab is no longer merely “summaries can lose information.” Its later workflow separates **selection from production**: the model selects source addresses for a question, and the host reconstructs exact evidence for the writing call.

That procedure produced useful results, although the later records also show that better selection coverage does not always produce better answers. The lesson is therefore not that this policy universally works. It is that **acquisition coverage, selection quality, and use of selected evidence are separate stages worth designing and observing**.  

E20 has the tools for part of this. `work_on_exact` (select specified exact source regions together) already exists, and the scripted qualification deliberately reselects the two editing targets after completing the eleven-file inspection. **What remains untested is whether Qwen constructs an appropriate evidence group itself, especially when the governing relationship extends beyond the edit target.** 

The coverage record answers:

> Has this version of this material appeared in an earlier input?

It does not answer:

> Which passages must be considered together to justify the current change?

That distinction should now drive the review. Suppose Qwen edits a boundary condition after releasing a file that defines the corresponding limit. The useful question is not simply whether that file was previously covered. It is whether the current exact sources or working account preserve the relationship needed for the edit.

**What to borrow:** the selection-to-exact-reconstruction pattern, not the document lab’s entire pipeline.

For a subsequent policy experiment, the actor could use its existing account to identify the current repair question, the relevant source references, and any unresolved dependency, then select those passages with the existing tools. It need not write a summary of every inspected file.

For the already qualified run, do not insert that instruction retrospectively. Observe whether the actor makes this transition unaided. If it does, keep the simpler policy. If it does not, the saved failure becomes a specific test of evidence selection—not another demonstration that “seen before” is insufficient.

## 2. Account staleness is a recurring known problem, not a new discovery waiting for E20

The main information lab’s September 24 repair addressed cases where navigation outcomes left the prompt while an account still described the operation as pending. Its successor preserves bounded recent navigation outcomes, separates action and companion-update results, and presents those outcomes before the account. It deliberately does not rewrite the model’s plans or infer comprehension. That repair was CPU-tested; its subsequent native behavior remained untested in the published record. 

The pressure project has its own current-result and history machinery, so I would not blindly copy that implementation. But E19 still finished with an account saying the policy file needed reading after the file had been read. The run succeeded because source and action evidence supplied alternative continuity. 

E20 inherits the account design: an optional account is recorded before an accompanying operation, and the current account remains the latest accepted model-authored text. The new coverage policy does not add semantic reconciliation of that account. 

That is not necessarily a host bug. Automatically rewriting an account would introduce another source of semantic error. **But leaving accounts optional and unchanged is not a tested account-maintenance policy either.**

What E20 should borrow immediately is the peer project’s **transition test**, not a universal update requirement:

> An account says an acquisition is pending. The acquisition succeeds or fails. Another operation follows. Does the next input make the actual outcome and the account’s historical timing unambiguous?

The behavioral review should then record whether Qwen updates the operative question, repeats completed work, or correctly relies on the new outcome despite the stale prose.

If stale accounts become consequential, test refreshing them at meaningful boundaries—after an uncertainty is resolved, a plan is contradicted, or the task changes phase. Do not respond by requiring an account rewrite after every operation.

The lesson already learned is **“a current account can describe an obsolete situation.”** The still-open question is which maintenance policy prevents that efficiently.

## 3. One concrete design choice repeats the permanent-bookkeeping tendency

The 3060 lab documented how always-resident state tables consumed space and how their presence did not guarantee useful use. Its mechanism review also corrected an overstatement: absence of explicit retrieval did not mean the records had no function. The proper question was what the displayed administrative information enabled relative to its cost. 

E20’s `coverage_policy.view()` (construct the model-facing coverage description) currently builds all eleven per-file rows on every call, including after the first accepted mutation has permanently closed the prerequisite. Those rows include identities, covered and missing counts, and current-version match status. The overall barrier becomes inactive, but the detailed table remains. 

That is mechanically truthful. It is also a candidate example of **preserving the full administrative view after its immediate job has ended**.

Once the required pre-edit exposure is complete, the actor usually needs something closer to:

> Required initial exposure completed before this recorded edit. That historical requirement remains satisfied. Current editing and check applicability are shown separately.

The exact per-file proof can remain stored and retrievable. Repeating the entire table is not necessary to preserve it.

I would treat this as a small successor presentation change, not a reason to invalidate the qualified run. Measure the actual contribution of that block before attributing a large cost to it. The initial prompt totals 6,738 tokens, but that total includes the entire interface and state—not just coverage. 

The transferable rule is:

> **Keep the historical record rich; keep the current presentation appropriate to the phase of work.**

That principle applies equally to completed inspection requirements, resolved questions, old failures, and already-consumed check results.

## 4. E20 must not treat another successful inspection as evidence about summary-based continuity

The long-horizon prototype already provides a decisive counterexample to a simplistic coverage theory. In its reviewed 75k run, nothing was removed. Qwen reached the correct strict-threshold conclusion repeatedly, omitted it from its saved finding, and reversed it in the final answer while the deciding sources were still present. 

The newer document-lab records similarly show that increasing the number of relevant passages presented can fail to improve—or can worsen—the resulting answer. More acquisition and broader selection are not equivalent to better integration. 

E20’s documents explicitly acknowledge these distinctions. Therefore, I would not accuse Codex of overlooking them conceptually. **The danger is programmatic: continually demonstrating exposure and ordinary repair while postponing the transition experiment that could resolve your central question.**

The new qualification establishes a researcher-selected feasible route through source replacement, editing, actual failed checks, correction, and submission. It does not establish model-selected grouping, account usefulness, or pressured recovery; the independent review says so. 

That qualification is necessary. It becomes diminishing-return work only if the programme keeps repeating similar qualifications without moving to the next question.

Once an unassisted E20 completion exists, preserve it as a reference. Then use a consequential checkpoint to test whether an actor-produced account and selected support can replace some of the previously available evidence. Follow that diagnostic with a fresh task run.

Do not force the present actor to make an error or discard sources merely to obtain a pressure story. Do not change its frozen task. But do give the subsequent continuity test an explicit place in the work queue.
